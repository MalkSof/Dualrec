One recording, four files

Each source is saved to its own file, in one folder per recording. They all start at the same instant and have the same length: drop them at the start of your timeline and they are already aligned.

screen.mp4

A full screen or a single window, at up to 60 frames per second.

camera.mp4

Your camera at full resolution, to place and crop wherever you want.

mic.m4a

Your voice on its own, to clean it up or boost it without touching the rest.

system.m4a

Whatever plays on the computer, separate from the microphone.

This is how they land in your editor: same start and end marks.

What people use it for

Tutorials and courses

Show the full screen when you explain a step and your face when you talk. You decide in the edit, not while recording.

Product demos

Record once and cut a horizontal version for YouTube and a vertical one for shorts, with the camera somewhere else.

Classes and presentations

Videos with clean audio

Built so you can record without thinking about recording

Floating camera bubble

See yourself while you record, move it out of the way and resize it with a double click.

The app stays out of the video

Dualrec and the bubble are left out of the screen recording. You can turn this off.

Shortcuts from any app

Ctrl + Alt + R starts or stops and Ctrl + Alt + P pauses, without going back to the Dualrec window.

Pause that keeps everything in sync

When you pause, all four tracks skip the same stretch. They are still aligned when you resume.

Uses your graphics card

Encodes H.264 in hardware with NVIDIA, Intel or AMD, and on the CPU when there is none.

If it closes, you keep what you recorded

Each track is written to disk from the first second and still plays even if the app closes halfway.

How it works

  1. Pick what to recordScreen or window, camera, microphone and system audio. Each one turns on or off on its own.
  2. Press RecordOr use the shortcut. Pause as many times as you want.
  3. Stop and open the folderThe files are in Videos\Dualrec, one folder per recording, ready for your editor.

Frequently asked questions

How do I record my screen and webcam separately?

Open Dualrec, pick the screen or window and the camera, and press Record. When you stop you get two separate videos, screen.mp4 and camera.mp4, plus the microphone and system audio as their own files.

Is Dualrec free?

Yes. Recording on your computer is free, with no sign-up and no watermark.

Do the tracks stay in sync?

Yes. All tracks share one clock: they start at the same instant and have the same length, so in your editor you just drop them at the start of the timeline and they line up.

Is it an alternative to Loom or OBS?

It covers the same job as a screen recorder with a webcam overlay, with a different result: instead of one video with the camera burned in, you get each source as its own file. It is simpler than OBS (no scenes to set up) and, unlike Loom, it records on your computer with no account.

How is it different from other screen recorders?

Most of them give you a single video with the camera already burned on top of the screen. Dualrec gives you each source as its own file, so you decide in the edit where the camera goes, when it shows and how each audio sounds.

Does it work on Mac?

For now Dualrec runs on Windows 10 and 11. A macOS version is planned.

Do I need internet to record?

No. Dualrec records offline and the files stay on your computer.

Which editors can open the recordings?

Any video editor. Videos are MP4 with H.264 and audio is M4A with AAC, formats that DaVinci Resolve, Premiere, CapCut and every common editor can open.
