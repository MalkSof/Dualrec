How to make vertical Shorts, Reels and TikToks from a screen recording

Your monitor is wide and a phone is tall. That is why a tutorial recorded on the desktop cannot go straight to YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels or TikTok: either it ends up tiny between two black bars, or you crop it and lose half the picture. And if the webcam was burned into a corner, the crop will probably take it out.

The way around it is to build the vertical video in your editor from loose parts: the screen on one side, your camera on the other. This post shows how to do it with a recording made of separate tracks.

Why a separate webcam file helps

Dualrec saves the screen as screen.mp4 and the webcam as camera.mp4, in sync and each at its full resolution. For vertical video that means three things:

  • Nothing sits on top of the screen, so you can zoom into any area.
  • The camera can be large, in its own part of the frame, without getting pixelated.
  • The same recording also gives you the horizontal version, with no second take.

Before you record: think vertical

A few choices while recording make the edit much easier:

  • Make things bigger. Raise the browser zoom or the font size of the app. Small text is unreadable on a phone.
  • Record a single window and make it narrower than usual: the less wide the content is, the less you have to crop.
  • Talk in short blocks. One idea per stretch, with a pause in between, so it is easy to cut standalone clips later.
  • Look at the camera when you open and close each idea: those are the moments when your face will fill most of the frame.

Step by step in the editor

Menu names vary from one program to another, but the process is the same in DaVinci Resolve, Premiere, CapCut or any editor with multiple tracks.

  1. Create a vertical project. Set the resolution to 1080 × 1920 pixels, which is a 9:16 aspect ratio.
  2. Import the files from the recording folder and drop them at the start of the timeline. They are already aligned.
  3. Pick the segment. Trim down to one idea that makes sense without context. Cut all the tracks together so they stay in sync.
  4. Put the screen on top. Scale it up until the area that matters fills the width, and crop whatever is left over on the sides.
  5. Put the camera below. Scale it to the width of the frame and crop it so your face is centered.
  6. Follow the action. If the important part moves elsewhere on the screen, reframe with a cut or a position animation.
  7. Balance the audio. Voice up front; system sound low or muted, as needed.
  8. Add captions. Plenty of people watch on their phones with the sound off. Several editors generate captions automatically, and you only have to fix them.
  9. Export as MP4 with H.264, at 1080 × 1920.

Three layouts that work

Screen on top, camera below

A classic layout for tutorials: viewers see what you are doing and who is explaining it at the same time. Split the frame in two halves, or give the screen more room.

Full-frame camera with cuts to the screen

Open by talking to the camera, which tends to be a stronger hook for the first few seconds, and cut to the screen when you show something. This only works if the camera is in its own file at a good resolution.

Full-frame screen with the camera in a circle

Useful when what you are showing needs all the space. The camera stays small, cropped to a circle, and you move it whenever it covers something.

Details that make a difference

  • Leave margins. The apps draw buttons, the account name and the caption over the video, mostly at the bottom and on the right. Keep important text out of those areas.
  • Lead with the result. Show what the viewer will get in the first few seconds, then explain how.
  • One topic per video. A twenty-minute recording can yield several short clips, each with a single idea.
  • Check it on your phone before you publish: that is where it will be watched.

One recording, several videos

The real advantage is that you are not recording for one format. The same four files give you the full horizontal video for YouTube and, by duplicating the timeline, the vertical cuts for social media. Frequently asked questions

What resolution should a vertical video be?

The usual choice is 1080 × 1920 pixels, a 9:16 aspect ratio. That is the format used by YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok.

Does Dualrec export vertical videos?

No. Dualrec records and gives you the separate files; you build the vertical video afterwards in your video editor.

Do I have to record twice, once for horizontal and once for vertical?

No. Because the screen and the camera are separate files, one recording is enough to build both versions.

Can I make a vertical video from a recording with the webcam already burned in?

You can crop it, but the camera stays where it was: it may get cut off, or cover exactly what you wanted to show. That is why it is worth recording the camera to a separate file.
