Loom solved one problem well: you record your screen with your face in a bubble, and when you stop you already have a link to send. For a quick message to a teammate it is hard to beat.

That same design works against you in other cases: when you want to edit the video, when you do not want to upload the recording anywhere, or when you do not want to depend on an account. Those cases call for a recorder that works on your computer. This article compares the two approaches and says plainly when each one is the better choice.

What a local recorder does differently

A recorder like Loom is built around the link: the video is uploaded to its servers and watched in the browser. A local recorder like Dualrec is built around the files: the recording stays on your disk and you decide what to do with it.

The practical differences follow from that:

  • No account. Dualrec installs and records. No sign-up, no email.
  • No connection needed. It records offline, and nothing is uploaded.
  • No time limit and no watermark. The limit is your disk space.
  • Each source as its own file. Instead of one video with the camera burned in, you get four synced files: screen.mp4, camera.mp4, mic.m4a and system.m4a.

That last point changes the result the most. With the camera as a separate file you can move it, resize it or remove it in the edit, and build a vertical video without the bubble covering what you are showing. It is explained in detail in why recording separate tracks makes editing easier.

When Loom is the better choice

  • You want to send the video right now, with a link, with no editing.
  • You work in a team and need comments, reactions and viewer tracking.
  • You record on a Mac or from the browser. Dualrec currently runs only on Windows 10 and 11.

When Dualrec is the better choice

  • You make tutorials, courses or content for YouTube, Instagram or TikTok, and the video gets edited before it is published.
  • You want one horizontal video and several vertical ones from the same footage. There is a guide to making Reels and Shorts from a screen recording.
  • You show information you do not want to upload to a third-party service.
  • You record long sessions: classes, demos or meetings longer than a few minutes.
  • You use Claude and want the AI to build the video for you. Dualrec ships with a connector for Claude that cuts the silences and exports the finished video.

What Dualrec does not have yet

To be direct about it: Dualrec does not generate a share link today. To send a recording you upload the file wherever you prefer (Drive, an unlisted YouTube video, a chat). The version with a link is in development, and the home page shows what is coming.

It also has no automatic transcription and no Mac version. Both are planned.

How to try it

  1. Download Dualrec and install it. It is free.
  2. Pick the screen or window, the camera and the microphone.
  3. Press Grabar (Record), or Ctrl + Alt + R, and when you are done press Cortar (Stop). The app interface is in Spanish for now.
  4. Open the recording folder: the four files are there, ready for your editor.

The full walkthrough is in how to record your screen and webcam separately on Windows.

Does Dualrec replace Loom?

It depends on the use. For recording videos that get edited afterwards, yes: it gives more control and needs no account. For sending a video instantly with a link, Loom is still more convenient, because Dualrec does not generate links yet.

Is it really free?

Yes. Recording on your computer is free, with no sign-up, no watermark and no time limit.

Can I share a Dualrec recording?

Yes, by uploading the file wherever you like: the videos are standard MP4 files. What is missing for now is an automatic link when you stop recording.

Does it work on Mac?

Not yet. Dualrec runs on Windows 10 and 11, and a macOS version is planned.
